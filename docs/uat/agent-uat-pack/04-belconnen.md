# Jules · Belconnen

**Six months between contracts**

You are a solo contractor looking at a one-bedroom apartment. You take home $9,700 a month while working and could be between contracts for six months. You have a 10% deposit, financed mortgage insurance and no second income, leave payout or redundancy payment. You are unsure how quickly this unit would sell.

### Make the decision as Jules

1. **Today's position.** Test the $600,000 offer, $97,500 cash before settlement and $4,000 total first-year works including urgent works with the original property checks. Find the monthly shortfall if your contract ends, the runway, the length of the interruption and net sale equity in the forced-sale scenario. What could make keeping the apartment difficult? What could make leaving difficult?
2. **A different resale assumption.** Under **Adjust property checks**, change only **Would this property be difficult to sell?** to **No**. Test. What changed in the sale price and net sale equity? Revisit the income-loss figures. Decide how much weight you would give an agent's assurance about resale.
3. **A smaller loan and more cash.** Set the difficult-to-sell answer back to **Yes**. Test an offer of $450,000, cash before settlement of $160,000 and total first-year works including urgent works of $9,000. Record net sale equity and cash after sale separately. Explain what each would mean if you had to move. What would you do about offering under this revised plan?
